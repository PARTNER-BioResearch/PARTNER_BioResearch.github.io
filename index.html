Shaping research that respects communities.

Why It Matters

Clinical research should be easier to understand and easier to access.

Many people never hear about clinical trials or feel unsure about what participation involves. P.A.R.T.N.E.R. BioResearch can use this page to communicate trust, answer common questions, and invite people into a more informed conversation.

Learn more about P.A.R.T.N.E.R. BioResearch.

I have started a workforce initiative starting with the tribes in Oklahoma and moving towards others. We as Native people have not wholly been in clinical research in 40 years. In fact we represent less than 1% of the population. That means our medicine being prescribed to us has a significant chance that it’s never been tested on a Native American patients. We wonder why the median life span is 46 years old for men. If we bring research into the tribe and it is successful for the patient, pharmaceuticals have approved the continued use even if the drug isn’t on the market yet. PARTNER is implementing a workforce initiative which is a clinical research training program for any federally recognized tribe and/or First Nation Tribe. Our program is accredited and will now bring in opportunities for tribal new hires/staff to become accredited clinical researcher coordinators and trained research PI’s (physicians) We will be covering costs of training for the first ten tribes we partner with. Funding for the training program for the tribe will be part of a grant that is currently being considered. We now will have tribal owned clinical research institutes for our people, with tribal data sovereignty, that is generating revenue for their people and communities.
